Article II · Mutual undertakings

Terms of Our Covenant

These vows are personal commitments, not a claim that any law automatically validates a marriage.

The promise of Mg Min Khant

I, Mg Min Khant, born on 6 January 2004, make these promises freely and with a sincere heart.

I promise to treat Mg Linn Thit Aung as my equal—to listen before judging, to speak honestly, and to protect the trust we build together.

I promise that love will never be an excuse for control, threats, humiliation, or harm. I will respect his boundaries, his dreams, his friendships, and his right to make choices for himself.

I will celebrate his happiness, stand beside him through difficulty, and work through disagreements with patience. When I make mistakes, I will take responsibility and try to repair the hurt I have caused.

I offer this promise not as ownership, but as a daily choice: to love, respect, and grow alongside him.

The promise of Mg Linn Thit Aung

I, Mg Linn Thit Aung, born on 25 April 2007, make these promises freely and with a sincere heart.

I promise to treat Mg Min Khant as my equal—to be truthful, to communicate with care, and to honour the confidence he places in me.

I promise to respect his individuality and boundaries, to support his hopes without demanding that he give up his own identity, and to make room for both of us to change and mature.

I will not use jealousy, silence, or anger to punish him. I will seek understanding when we disagree, apologise when I am wrong, and remember that a healthy love makes both people feel safe.

I choose to build a relationship grounded in kindness, loyalty, freedom, and mutual respect—one honest day at a time.

Our shared covenant

We promise to keep learning about one another, to make important decisions through honest conversation, and to respect each other's consent in every part of our relationship.

We will not measure our love by perfection. We will measure it by how we care, how we repair, and how we allow each other to be fully human.

We understand that this promise cannot replace local legal requirements, nor can it remove either person's right to safety, dignity, or freedom. We make it as a meaningful record of the love and commitment we wish to honour.

Article III · Guiding principles

Rights, Dignity & Respect

Our commitments are inspired by widely recognised human-rights principles. These references are educational context, not a legal opinion or proof of marriage registration.

01 · Equality and dignity

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR), especially Articles 1 and 2, sets out the principle that people are born free and equal in dignity and rights and should enjoy rights without prohibited discrimination. We commit to treating each other with equal dignity.

02 · Privacy and personal life

UDHR Article 12 recognises protection against arbitrary interference with privacy and family life. We commit to respecting each other's privacy and to discussing boundaries rather than using surveillance or coercion.

03 · Free and full consent

UDHR Article 16 describes marriage as requiring the free and full consent of the intending spouses. We affirm that any commitment must be freely chosen, without pressure, threats, or manipulation.

04 · Equal protection and non-discrimination

The International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR), including Articles 2 and 26, sets out obligations concerning rights and equality before the law. The application of these protections to particular circumstances depends on treaty obligations and legal interpretation in each jurisdiction.

05 · Freedom from harm

We recognise each person's right to personal safety and dignity. Love does not justify violence, intimidation, forced disclosure, or controlling behaviour. We agree to seek help and prioritise safety if a relationship becomes harmful.

06 · Our own responsibility

We understand that legal recognition of same-sex marriage differs between jurisdictions and may change over time. This keepsake is a personal declaration only; it is not a government certificate, civil registration, or guarantee of legal rights in any country.

Reference texts to consult: United Nations, Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948), Articles 1, 2, 12 and 16; International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (1966), Articles 2 and 26. Official text: UDHR — United Nations · ICCPR — OHCHR.
